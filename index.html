About Me

Hello! I am an M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering with a concentration in AI/ML Systems at Carnegie Mellon University. I received my B.S. in Computer Sciences and Data Science from the University of Wisconsin-Madison. I currently work on research projects advised by Professor Nikolas Martelaro at Carnegie Mellon University and Professor Steven Dow at the University of California, San Diego. Previously, I worked on a project advised by Professor Aniket Kittur at Carnegie Mellon University. As an undergraduate, I worked with Professor Jacob Thebault-Spieker at the University of Wisconsin-Madison.

My research interests are Human-Computer Interaction and Human-AI Interaction. I build mixed-initiative AI systems that infer people's processes and intentions from their activities (e.g., design), and use this understanding to decide when to intervene and what support to offer, while minimizing disruption and preserving people's agency and ownership of their thinking. I am also interested in incorporating human expertise (e.g., tacit knowledge) into AI systems and developing personalized AI assistance that adapts to people's individual needs, preferences, and working styles.

Outside of research, I love weightlifting and cooking.

Investigating How Designers Perceive the Timing and Content of Proactive AI-generated Feedback Co-second author

Wizard-of-Oz Mixed-Initiative Interaction Proactive AI Design
Howard Ziyu Han, Sirui Tao*, Jingwen Pan*, Seung Heon Song*, Steven P. Dow, Nikolas Martelaro
* indicates equal contribution
Under review at the ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '27)
Imagine you are working to solve a design problem. An AI assistant watches your work and proactively provides feedback to help you (yay!). When should the assistant intervene, and what feedback should it provide? We created a Wizard-of-Oz system where designers received in-situ, AI-generated perspectives, mediated by a human wizard. In a study with N=20 designers, participants rated each intervention for timeliness and usefulness. Our study offers implications for future autonomous proactive design support.

Visualizing Analogical Innovation with Generative AI

Analogical Reasoning Generative AI
Imagine you are on a team designing laundry solution that uses less water. You draw analogical inspiration from mycelium foams that absorb oil films in polluted water, and propose a waterless cleaning patch that uses mycelium to lift and isolate dirt through physical adsorption. This project builds a system that generates images that illustrate these analogical innovation ideas through image scaffolding and that inspire new ideas.

Understanding User Perceptions of Safety Information in Navigational Tools

Explainable Interfaces Navigation Systems
Rejected by the ACM Conference on Computer-Supported Cooperative Work and Social Computing (CSCW)
Note: Inspired by my colleague Sirui, I include the rejected paper.
Imagine you open your phone's navigation app to find a route to your destination. The app shows you routes with safety information and explanations. How do you perceive the safety information, and how does it influence your route choice? How do different explanations influence your perception? We investigated these questions through a mixed-methods study with 31 participants and provided insights for future navigation systems.
